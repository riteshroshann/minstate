# Spot training still ships every byte of Adam

Every published system that keeps a deep-learning job alive through preemption, resizing or failure moves **the complete training state: weights plus both Adam moments**. They replicate it, shadow it, recompute it, checkpoint it or migrate it whole. None of them asks which parts actually have to cross. A paper that measures the minimum optimizer state a migrating job must carry is therefore novel in its core question, but only if the claim is stated narrowly. Two 2026 arXiv preprints come close. Mitra (arXiv 2609.22087) shows that dropping AdamW state at an interruption costs measurable perplexity and that rebuilding a decayed first moment recovers only about 21% of the loss. Zhang et al. (arXiv 2611.00589) compress Adam moments under a first-update guarantee and frame checkpoints as the way runs survive "preemption and migration". Neither paper measures steps-to-recovery against a deterministic control, splits m from v at equal bytes, rebuilds v at the destination, or prices payload bytes against a notice window. The standard to match is set by systems papers (throughput, cost, recovery time and ETTR on replayed spot traces, usually with no error bars) and ML papers (3 to 5 seeds, mean ± std). The new paper should beat both by reporting seed-banded, censored recovery metrics alongside measured bytes and drain times. Cross-device claims hold up for CPU, CUDA and Intel XPU on one host, and for TPU only if a run is actually executed. Laptop NPUs cannot train at all, so no migration claim involving them is possible. The best journal for the work at its current scale is **TMLR**, which is free, double-blind and does not require novelty. **IEEE TPDS** is the best IEEE choice if multi-node experiments are added, and **IEEE TCC** is the IEEE fallback.

## One page: what the field has, what it lacks, where to send it

Spot-training research has spent five years on two questions: how to reconfigure parallelism when machines vanish, and when to buy spot versus on-demand capacity. Varuna, Bamboo, Oobleck, Parcae, ReCycle, Lazarus and TrainMover answer the first. SkyPilot, Can't Be Late, SkyNomad and GFS answer the second. Checkpointing research has run in parallel. It moved from frequency tuning (CheckFreq) to asynchronous multi-level I/O (DataStates-LLM), in-memory and peer copies (Gemini, Checkmate), load-time resharding (ByteCheckpoint, Universal Checkpointing) and lossy size reduction (Check-N-Run, Inshrinkerator, ExCP, BitSnap, MoC-System, MoEvement). Across both lines, the optimizer state is cargo that must arrive intact. Even the most recent "how to move state" paper, ETC (July 2026), states that "it is necessary to migrate model parameters, optimizer states and other states" ([arXiv 2607.04749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04749)). The schedulers model recovery as a constant. Can't Be Late folds launch time, setup and lost progress into a single "changeover delay d" ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/wu-zhanghao)).

On the ML side, a body of 2018-2025 optimizer work shows that much of Adam's second moment is redundant. Adafactor keeps only row and column sums ([arXiv 1804.04235](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235)). Adam-mini removes "≥ 99.9%" of v's per-coordinate learning rates ([arXiv 2406.16793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16793)). SlimAdam compresses up to 98% of second moments and identifies the embedding token dimension as the part that resists compression ([arXiv 2503.01843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01843)). Separately, warm-up and bias-correction theory explains why a freshly zeroed Adam misbehaves. But these papers keep a compressed v throughout training. None of them studies a v that is dropped, factored or rebuilt **once, at a migration boundary**.

The new paper sits in that gap. The defensible novelty statement is: *the first step-resolved ablation, measured as steps-to-recovery and regret against a bit-deterministic control with a seed-band noise floor, of which Adam state components (θ, m, v, factored or low-rank v, embedding-only v) must cross a preemption boundary, priced in measured bytes and fed into a notice-window, bandwidth and spot-price migration policy.* Mitra 2026 and Zhang et al. 2026 must be cited as concurrent work and differentiated explicitly.

On devices, the paper can honestly claim migration among CPU, CUDA and Intel XPU on a single host, validated against both a seed band and a measured device band. TPU is possible through a free Kaggle session but must be executed before it is mentioned. NPU migration is impossible. On venue, TMLR rewards exactly what the project does well (controls, noise floors, censored metrics, honest extrapolation labels) and penalises nothing it lacks except unsupported claims. TPDS reviewers would weight scale most heavily, and scale is the project's weakest point.

## The twelve papers this work builds on

The twelve anchors below favour 2023-2026 work at top venues and labs and include both close competitors. For each, the paragraph gives the citation, affiliation, venue, what it established, what it left undone, and how the new paper relates to it. BibTeX keys refer to `refs.bib` in the notes folder.

### 1. Mitra 2026, the closest competitor (`mitra2026orbittrace`)

Subhadip Mitra, "When Is Availability-Aware Training Worth It? A Benchmark and Empirical Study of Interruption-Resilient Optimization Under Predictable Compute Schedules", arXiv 2609.22087, June 2026. Single author; the notes do not record an affiliation. Peer-review status is unknown. On power- or downlink-constrained nodes it treats a weights-only checkpoint as the realistic choice and asks how much pre-gap moment to trust on resume. **A full-state checkpoint with an effective-time LR schedule matched uninterrupted GPT-2/AdamW training exactly (24.863 ± 0.019 perplexity both ways). Dropping AdamW state cost +0.76 (short) and +0.32 (long) perplexity. Reconstructing a decayed first moment recovered only about 21% of that penalty, not robustly across seeds**, and a single seed gave 38.8% against the five-seed mean. On CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, full-state checkpointing scored 86.07 ± 0.31 against 85.50 ± 0.28 for weights-only. It ran 3 seeds (CIFAR) and 5 seeds (GPT-2) on one A100 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22087); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22087)). It did not account for bytes. It measured only end-of-run quality, with no recovery-transient metric. It did not test quantized or factorized moments, contrast w_v with w_m, or re-estimate moments with frozen gradient passes, and it had no bit-deterministic control: its data-iterator re-creation "adds order noise". The new paper differs on four axes. It measures steps-to-recovery and regret rather than final perplexity. It splits m from v and tests factored and embedding-only v. It rebuilds v at the destination with a frozen warm-up instead of decaying a cached moment. And it prices bytes and feeds a migration policy. Mitra's single-seed versus five-seed gap is also useful evidence for the project's own rule that single-seed results are not results.

### 2. Zhang et al. 2026, the compression-side competitor (`zhang2026guarded`)

Annan Zhang, Shengjie Song, Wenshuai Yao, Lihao Zhang and Tianmiao (as listed on arXiv), "Guarded Compression of Adam Optimizer State with Shared-Gradient Bounds", arXiv 2611.00589, posted 31 October 2026. Affiliation not recorded in the notes. A compressed coordinate is accepted only if the restored first Adam update stays within a tolerance t in LR units. **Guarded 8-bit log codes cut moment payloads by 69.5% on OPT-125M and 71.9% on ResNet-18 relative to FP32, and "every restored first update stays within tolerance."** Evaluation covered 4 seeds × 3 training fractions × 2 models (120 restores) on an H100, with codec timing over 20 warm-ups and 100 rounds, and development loss measured after 100-update continuations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00589)). Its introduction frames checkpoints as how runs "survive failures, preemption and migration". It does not test dropping moments, rebuilding them, or any bytes-versus-recovery trade-off beyond compression. The new paper should put its payload on the same Pareto plot, adopt its first-update error as a secondary diagnostic, and contrast "compress the moments" with "rebuild the moments". Its evaluation protocol (seeds × fractions × models, warmed-up timing) is the most rigorous in the notes and is the template to match.

### 3. ETC, the newest full-state migration system (`liu2026etc`)

Weijian Liu, Mingzhen Li, Rui Kang, Chen Sun, Guangming Tan and Weile Jia, "Direct Model State Migration for Elastic Training of Large Language Models", arXiv 2607.04749, July 2026. ICT, Chinese Academy of Sciences; it shares an author with EasyScale. ETC does checkpoint-free peer-to-peer state migration for elastic Megatron-LM and **reduces migration overhead by 2.33-6.37× relative to checkpoint-based approaches** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04749)). It states that migrating optimizer states is necessary and does not test that premise. The new paper tests it directly. A reduced payload is complementary to ETC's transport, because ETC's speed-up applies to whatever bytes are chosen. Cite ETC as the clearest recent statement of the assumption under test.

### 4. Parcae, the state-of-the-art spot-training system (`duan2024parcae`)

Jiangfei Duan, Ziang Song, Xupeng Miao, Xiaoli Xi, Dahua Lin, Harry Xu, Minjia Zhang and Zhihao Jia, "Parcae: Proactive, Liveput-Optimized DNN Training on Preemptible Instances", NSDI 2024. CMU, CUHK, UCLA and Microsoft Research. Parcae predicts availability with ARIMA, optimises "liveput" (expected throughput under possible preemptions) and keeps a CPU-side copy of "parameters and optimizer states" that "reduces communication by 5× for stateful optimizers (e.g., Adam)". **It reports up to 10× over prior spot systems and 2.4× over Bamboo and Varuna on GPT-2 with 32 spot V100s** ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/duan); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14097)). Evaluation replays a 12-hour trace from 32 p3.2xlarge instances on ResNet-152, VGG-19, BERT, GPT-2 and GPT-3, against Varuna, Bamboo, on-demand and an oracle, measuring tokens/s and USD per token. No repetitions or error bars were found in the text. Parcae's migration ships full state, and its CPU shadow exists because Adam's moments are treated as mandatory. The new paper's result answers how much of that shadow is needed. Parcae's trace-replay design, liveput and cost-per-token metrics are the systems vocabulary to adopt.

### 5. Can't Be Late, the decision frame (`wu2024cantbelate`)

Zhanghao Wu, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ziming Mao, Zongheng Yang, Eric Friedman, Scott Shenker and Ion Stoica, "Can't Be Late: Optimizing Spot Instance Savings under Deadlines", NSDI 2024, Outstanding Paper. UC Berkeley Sky Computing Lab. It proposes the parameter-free "Uniform Progress" policy for switching between spot and on-demand capacity under deadlines. **It is evaluated on three months of open-sourced AWS spot availability traces, with 27-84% savings on SkyPilot.** Recovery is a constant "changeover delay d, which includes the time required to launch an instance, set up dependencies, and recover any potential progress loss caused by gaps between checkpoints" ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/wu-zhanghao)). It does not model how d depends on what state is carried. The new paper turns d into a measured function of payload choice, bandwidth and notice window. Its public traces are the obvious input for the policy evaluation. SkyNomad (Berkeley, arXiv 2601.06520) extends the frame to multiple regions with an explicit migration cost and 1.25-3.96× real-cloud savings ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06520)), and should be cited alongside it.

### 6. EasyScale, the consistency methodology (`li2023easyscale`)

Mingzhen Li, Wencong Xiao, Hailong Yang, Biao Sun, Hanyu Zhao, Shiru Ren, Zhongzhi Luan, Xianyan Jia, Yi Liu, Yong Li, Wei Lin and Depei Qian, "EasyScale: Elastic Training with Consistent Accuracy and Improved Utilization on GPUs", SC 2023. Beihang University and Alibaba. EasyScale demands that elastic training "generate bitwise identical model parameters compared to the non-elastic DDP training over a fixed number of GPUs". It traces non-determinism through the stack, shows TorchElastic and Pollux giving different ResNet-18 accuracies on 1-8 V100s with default seeds, and **improved cluster utilisation by 62.1% in deployment** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14228); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3581784.3607054)). Determinism replaces repetition in its evaluation. It covers heterogeneous GPU types only, and never changes what state crosses a resize. The new paper's step-keyed data and RNG invariants apply the same idea to migration. Citing EasyScale makes "only the carried state changes" legible to systems reviewers, and the methodology section should say so.

### 7. ExCP, the venue-published moment-compression precedent (`li2024excp`)

Wenshuo Li, Xinghao Chen, Han Shu, Yehui Tang and Yunhe Wang, "ExCP: Extreme LLM Checkpoint Compression via Weight-Momentum Joint Shrinking", ICML 2024 (oral per the arXiv comment). Huawei Noah's Ark Lab. It jointly prunes weights and both Adam moments, then applies non-uniform 4-bit quantization. **Pythia-410M shrinks from 4.53 GB to 0.06 GB (about 70×) with downstream average 43.11 → 42.93; PanGu-π-7B shrinks from 98.59 GB to 4.10 GB (about 25×) with average 56.71 → 57.09.** Its full text argues that momentum states "are only weakly related to the parameters in the previous checkpoint after updating for hundreds to thousands of steps, especially for the first-order moment whose β1 = 0.9" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11257); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11257)). It validates by final accuracy after periodic restores, and no seeds or error bars were found. It compresses rather than drops, and never measures a recovery transient. ExCP's horizon argument is the published version of the new paper's w_v-versus-w_m hypothesis. Its prune-small-v rule is a candidate arm, and its payload belongs on the Pareto plot.

### 8. Checkmate, rebuilding state from gradients (`bhardwaj2025checkmate`)

Ankit Bhardwaj, Weiyang Wang, Jeremy Carin, Adam Belay and Manya Ghobadi, "Checkmate: Zero-Overhead Model Checkpointing via Network Gradient Replication", arXiv 2507.13522, 2025. MIT. Switches multicast gradients to a CPU shadow cluster that replays the optimizer. **It reports 5-34.5× more frequent checkpointing, an 80-97.1% reduction in repeated work per failure, and 1.3-6.5× throughput at equal frequency** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13522)). It keeps the full optimizer state alive, just elsewhere. The new paper's frozen warm-up is the mirror image: it rebuilds moments at the destination from fresh gradients instead of maintaining them continuously. Checkmate's "repeated work per failure" is the systems-side twin of steps-to-recovery multiplied by step time, and the paper should report in that unit too.

### 9. Kokolis et al., the reliability and ETTR standard (`kokolis2025reliability`)

Apostolos Kokolis, Michael Kuchnik, John Hoffman, Adithya Kumar, Parth Malani, Faye Ma, Zachary DeVito, Shubho Sengupta, Kalyan Saladi and Carole-Jean Wu, "Revisiting Reliability in Large-Scale Machine Learning Research Clusters", HPCA 2025. Meta. **It analysed 11 months of data from two ML environments covering 4 million jobs and over 150 million A100 GPU hours**, fitted an MTTF model against scale, and introduced the Effective Training Time Ratio ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/hpca61900.2025.00096)). It measures failure, not what recovery should carry. The new paper should express its policy output as ETTR, or ETTR loss, under realistic failure and preemption rates, so that its numbers sit beside Kokolis and MoEvement.

### 10. MoEvement, partial-state checkpoints with an ETTR bar (`gandhi2026moevement`)

Swapnil Gandhi and Christos Kozyrakis, "Sparse Checkpointing for Fast and Reliable MoE Training", NSDI 2026. Stanford. It takes sparse expert snapshots plus upstream logging. **It reports up to 4× lower checkpoint overhead and 31× lower recovery overhead, with "ETTR ≥ 0.94 even under" an MTBF as low as 10 minutes** ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/gandhi); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15411)). Together with MoC-System (ASPLOS 2025, "up to a 98.9% reduction in overhead for each checkpointing process"; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04307)), it is the strongest venue-published precedent that not every piece of state must be saved at every checkpoint. Both papers drop experts, not optimizer moments, and both are MoE-specific. The new paper generalises the "partial state" idea to Adam's moments in dense models and should cite both as precedent.

### 11. CRIUgpu, the transparent "ship everything" upper bound (`stoyanov2025criugpu`)

Radostin Stoyanov, Viktória Spišaková, Jesus Ramos, Steven Gurfinkel, Andrei Vagin, Adrian Reber, Wesley Armour and Rodrigo Bruno, "CRIUgpu: Transparent Checkpointing of GPU-Accelerated Workloads", arXiv 2502.16631, 2025. Oxford, NVIDIA, Red Hat and others. Built on the driver's cuda-checkpoint, it supports CUDA and ROCm with no steady-state overhead. **In-memory checkpoint and restore on an H100 average 4.9 s and 2.5 s for GPT-2 Small, and 28 s and 11 s for GPT-2 XL (1.5B)**, across BERT-B to LLaMA 3.1 8B on H100, A100 and V100 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16631)). Transparent C/R snapshots all device memory, so its payload is at least the 16 B/param resident set plus activations. Application-level `full` ships 12 B/param. It is the upper bound the new paper's reduced payloads should be compared against. As an inference, not a measurement: GPT-2 XL's 28-second in-memory checkpoint already approaches a 30-second notice window before any network transfer. PhoenixOS (SOSP 2025) is the concurrent-C/R counterpart and should be cited with it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12079)).

### 12. SlimAdam, which tensors need their v (`kalra2025slimadam`)

Dayal Singh Kalra, John Kirchenbauer, Maissam Barkeshli and Tom Goldstein, "When Can You Get Away with Low Memory Adam?", arXiv 2503.01843, 2025. Affiliations not recorded in the notes. It defines a layer-wise signal-to-noise ratio for v along fan-in or fan-out dimensions and **saves up to 98% of total second moments**. Embedding and LM-head second moments "show a strong aversion to compressing along the token dimension (vocabulary dimension)", and attention and final norms lose SNR during training ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01843)). It maintains compressed v for the whole run and never transfers a v that was factored once at a boundary. Its SNR analysis predicts exactly where the new paper's `w_factored_v` residual should concentrate, and it gives the published rationale for the `w_emb_v` arm. Per-tensor-class results should be framed as a test of that prediction.

### Older and supporting work that must still be cited

Several classics are expected by any reviewer. Adafactor (Shazeer and Stern, ICML 2018) is the source of the rank-1 v estimator and of the observation that a slow second-moment decay "can produce larger-than-desired updates" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04235)). CheckFreq (FAST 2021) is the canonical frequency-and-overhead paper, with "recovery time from hours to seconds" at at most 3.5% overhead ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast21/presentation/mohan)). Varuna (EuroSys 2022) and Bamboo (NSDI 2023) are the spot-training baselines; Bamboo also supplies the trace-segmenting evaluation template and its "three times" repetition ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12013)). Pollux (OSDI 2021) represents the "adapt and accept a different trajectory" camp ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi21/presentation/qiao)). Check-N-Run (NSDI 2022) is the precedent for lossy, differential checkpoints ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi22/presentation/eisenman)). Gemini (SOSP 2023) and Just-In-Time Checkpointing (EuroSys 2024) are the fast-recovery baselines; JIT's checkpoint-at-failure is the analogue of draining during a notice window ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3627703.3650085)). Singularity (Microsoft, 2022) reports "tens of seconds" migration latency for transparent preemption ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07848)). 8-bit optimizers (Dettmers et al., ICLR 2022) and 4-bit states (Li, Chen, Zhu, NeurIPS 2023, including the "zero point problem" for v) are prior art for quantized moments ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02861); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01507)). For the transient, Ma and Yarats (AAAI 2021) tie warm-up to update magnitude and recommend 2/(1−β2) warm-up steps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04209)), Kalra and Barkeshli (NeurIPS 2024) propose a v initialisation that substitutes for warm-up ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09405)), and Laing and Orvieto (2025) recast bias correction as an implicit LR schedule ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20516)). Summers and Dinneen (ICML 2021) justify the seed and device bands: "even one-bit changes in initial parameters result in models converging to vastly different values" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04514)). The original Adam and AdamW papers are not in the notes. They must be added to the bibliography by hand.

## Novelty survives, but only stated precisely

The searches in the notes ran the arXiv API with combinations of "optimizer state" plus preemption, spot instances, elastic training and lossy checkpoints, and found nothing on point. They found no paper that measures a bytes-versus-steps-to-recovery curve across partial optimizer payloads. No paper contrasts shipping m only with shipping v only at equal bytes. No paper rebuilds v by frozen-weight gradient accumulation at the destination, studies factored v as a migration payload, or breaks optimizer-state importance at a restart down by tensor class. No migration policy trades payload bytes against training steps lost under a notice-window deadline.

The near-misses fall into five groups, and each must be differentiated in one sentence in the related-work section. **Mitra 2026** measured the end-of-run cost of dropping AdamW state and tried decayed-m reconstruction; it measured no transient, no bytes, no m/v split and used no deterministic control. **Zhang et al. 2026** compresses moments with a guaranteed first update but never drops or rebuilds them. **ExCP, Inshrinkerator and BitSnap** report compression ratio against final accuracy after lossy restores, not step-resolved recovery. Inshrinkerator withstands "up to 10 restores with negligible accuracy impact" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11800)). **LayerCheck** (CLUSTER 2026) is the nearest on metric: it reports a post-restart loss deviation "by at most 0.54%" from the failure-free run with mixed-timestamp per-layer state, but it keeps the per-layer optimizer state rather than dropping moments ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27193)). **Asadi et al. (NeurIPS 2023)** reset Adam deliberately in deep RL, where the loss changes and stale moments hurt ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833)). That is the opposite regime to a stationary objective interrupted by migration, and the contrast is worth a sentence. Topollai and Choromanska (2026) study stale quantized states and resets in LLaMA-60M ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16731)), and Gupta et al. and Ibrahim et al. study LR re-warming when continuing pre-training ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763)). Whether either re-warming study resets optimizer state was not checked.

Two cautions keep the claim honest. First, Mitra already shows the headline intuition, that dropped state costs something and naive reconstruction recovers little. The new paper cannot claim to discover that. It can claim to quantify it per component, in steps and bytes, against a proper noise floor. Second, Zhang et al. was posted eleven days before these notes were compiled. A further competitor could appear before submission, and the search should be rerun immediately before the paper goes out.

Three things follow. The model scale of the ML-side competitors (OPT-125M, GPT-2 small, ResNet-18 on a single A100 or H100) is close to what a laptop can run, so scale alone is not disqualifying for an ML venue. Reviewers will still ask for at least one model at about 100M parameters or more. GPT-2 small (124M) needs about 2 GB of resident fp32 Adam state and should fit in 6 GB with bf16 autocast and gradient accumulation, according to the journal note's arithmetic. Second, the minimum credible statistics are 3-5 seeds with dispersion. Zhang et al.'s seeds × fractions × models grid sets the current high bar. Third, the systems metrics a TPDS or TCC reader expects are payload bytes, stall and save/load time, recovery time, ETTR or wasted GPU-hours, and cost on a replayed trace.

The checklist below is what the new paper must therefore report. Each item is grounded in an anchor's practice or in the project's own invariants.

| Item | Requirement | Anchor or reason |
|---|---|---|
| Noise floor | Seed band from ≥ 5 same-device controls, `eps` reported beside every StR, per scale and migration point | Mitra's single-seed vs five-seed gap; Summers and Dinneen |
| Primary metric | Steps-to-recovery on a fixed held-out batch set, with K = 50 consecutive in-band steps; censored values written as >W | Not reported by any anchor; this is the contribution |
| Secondary metric | Regret area over the window; report disagreements with StR | Robust to single crossings |
| Uncertainty | Bootstrap confidence intervals over seeds per arm at the headline cell; share of censored runs | TMLR reviewers' first question |
| Bytes | Measured serialized payload per arm in B/param, cross-checked against the analytic table | Zhang et al.'s payload %; CRIUgpu's upper bound |
| Latency breakdown | Serialize, transfer or drain (modelled from bandwidth, labelled as a model), deserialize, rebuild (frozen warm-up compute), recovery (StR × step time) | TrainMover attributes 35.1% of a 4.45-minute initialisation to checkpoint loading ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12636)) |
| Steady-state overhead | Cost of any per-step bookkeeping a payload choice requires, as % of iteration time | CheckFreq's ≤ 3.5% framing |
| Timing hygiene | Warmed-up timing runs with repeated rounds, outliers reported, no interleaved GPU work | Zhang et al.'s 20 warm-ups + 100 rounds |
| Diagnostics | First-update error after restore, per tensor class | Zhang et al.; SlimAdam's per-class prediction |
| Baselines on the Pareto plot | `full`, `w_only`, `cold_reset`, guarded or blockwise 8-bit moments, rank-1 factored v, and full checkpointing at reduced frequency | ExCP, Zhang et al., Adafactor |
| Systems-unit outputs | Repeated work per failure, ETTR under stated MTBF or preemption rate, cost on public AWS traces | Checkmate, Kokolis, MoEvement, Can't Be Late |
| Sensitivity | β2 ∈ {0.95, 0.99, 0.999} with matching β1 variation; at least early, mid and late migration points | Tests the horizon argument behind w_v vs w_m |
| Scale | At least one cell at ~124M; every 7B number labelled as extrapolation | Reviewer expectation at every venue |
| Reproducibility | A manifest per run, the git commit, and a DOI-backed artifact | TPDS badging rules require a persistent DOI ([TPDS](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/misc/299908)) |

## What can and cannot be claimed across device classes

The documentation is unambiguous on the main point. **No mainstream stack can train on a laptop NPU.** Intel's OpenVINO documents its NPU under "Running Inference" with "Supported Inference Data Types … F32, F16 … U8" and states that "computation precision for the HW is FP16" ([OpenVINO](https://docs.openvino.ai/2025/openvino-workflow/running-inference/inference-devices-and-modes/npu-device.html)). The Intel NPU Acceleration Library was archived on 24 April 2025 with an end-of-life notice ([GitHub](https://github.com/intel/intel-npu-acceleration-library)). AMD's Ryzen AI software is described as tools for "deploying AI inference" ([Ryzen AI](https://ryzenai.docs.amd.com/en/latest/)). Qualcomm's HTP backend in ONNX Runtime is documented for inference ([ORT QNN](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/QNN-ExecutionProvider.html)). Qualcomm-affiliated authors write that most low-power NPUs "are designed as fixed-point inference accelerators, without training capabilities" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04036)). The only published NPU "training" either bypasses the vendor stack through private Apple ANE APIs (Orion: a 110M transformer, 1,000 steps in 22 minutes; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06728)) or avoids backpropagation with zeroth-order methods (QScheduler; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18802)). Apple documents `MLUpdateTask` for updatable Core ML models, but does not say which compute unit runs it ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreml/mlupdatetask)). The paper therefore cannot claim GPU-to-NPU migration in any form. At most, the NPU can serve as an inference-only evaluator of migrated weights, and the paper should say so if it uses it at all. Huawei Ascend "NPUs" are datacentre training accelerators, a different device class. They must never share a sentence with laptop NPUs without that distinction.

**TPU is supportable only with an executed run.** JAX lists Cloud TPU support on Linux ([JAX](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/installation.html)). PyTorch/XLA is deprecated and its repository is archived on 15 November 2026 in favour of TorchTPU (`torch_tpu`, `device("tpu")`), which "supports standard PyTorch (ATen) operators in forward and backward passes" ([pytorch/xla](https://github.com/pytorch/xla); [Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/tpu/docs/pytorch-on-tpu)). Kaggle's TPU documentation, seen only as a search snippet, offers up to 20 hours a week and 9 hours per session ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/docs/tpu)). Colab offers free TPUs of unstated generation ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)). Whether `torch_tpu` installs on Kaggle is untested. A JAX/Optax route is a **cross-framework** migration, not the same job on a TPU. Optax's Adam state `(count, mu, nu)` maps to PyTorch's `(step, exp_avg, exp_avg_sq)` ([Optax](https://optax.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/transformations.html)), but three details must be handled and unit-tested on CPU first. Linear weight layouts are transposed (PyTorch out×in, Flax in×out), so the moments must transpose with them. `step` maps to `count`. Optax places eps outside the square root with an extra `eps_root` inside, so `eps_root=0` is needed to match PyTorch. Getting any of these wrong silently changes the optimizer and breaks the single-variable invariant.

**CPU, CUDA and Intel XPU are supportable on one host.** PyTorch 2.15 documents XPU training on Meteor Lake-H integrated graphics under Windows 11, which is this laptop's iGPU ([PyTorch XPU](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/notes/get_start_xpu.html)). The honest framing is that a device switch is itself a perturbation. PyTorch states that results "may not be reproducible between CPU and GPU executions, even when using identical seeds" ([PyTorch](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/notes/randomness.html)). Implementation-level variance alone reaches 2.9% accuracy and 52.4% per-class accuracy differences ([Pham et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/3324884.3416545)). Determinism can cost up to 746% overhead on some GPU architectures ([Zhuang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11872)). Bitwise cross-hardware replay has been claimed only with purpose-built ordered kernels (OPEN-1B; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17380)). No verified paper reports step-level loss divergence of the same training state continued on CPU versus GPU, so this is a small gap the project can fill.

The recommended measurement design has three bands and a cheap bitwise tier. The **seed band** comes from five or more same-device controls. The **device band** continues one `full` checkpoint at step T on each destination device with the same seed and batches, and takes the pairwise held-out-loss spread. The **replay band** continues identical state twice on the same device, and should be about zero on CUDA with deterministic algorithms enabled. Migration cells then cover cuda→cpu, cpu→cuda, cuda→xpu, xpu→cuda and cpu→xpu, crossed with `full`, `w_v`, `w_m` and `w_only` and with cold and frozen-warm-up protocols. StR and regret are reported against the same-device control, with both bands alongside. "Cross-device migration works" should mean that for `full` the gap stays within the seed band across the window and the device band is at or below the seed band, and that for reduced arms the device does not shift StR beyond what the device band explains.

Five controls remove confounds. Run all cross-device cells in fp32, because autocast op lists differ per device and XPU autocast is marked experimental ([torch.amp](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/amp.html)). Verify identical wire bytes by CRC. Draw batch indices and dropout masks on CPU. Fix the PyTorch version: the notes record the CUDA environment on 2.14 and the XPU environment on 2.15, so cuda↔xpu cells currently change both device and framework version and must be labelled that way until aligned. Finally, compute one Adam update from a fixed batch on both devices and report max-abs and relative differences per tensor class. Overclaiming would include "GPU → NPU" in any wording, "GPU cluster" for single-host multi-process runs, "TPU" without an executed run, "bitwise-equivalent" or "lossless" across devices, and DirectML as a measured device when it is an untested public preview ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/directml/pytorch-windows)).

## TMLR first, TPDS if the scale grows, TCC as the IEEE fallback

The journals were judged on scope, standing, rules, cost and the risk that reviewers sink the paper on scale. The ranked top three follow, then the requirements for each, and finally the alternatives that were ruled out.

**First: Transactions on Machine Learning Research.** TMLR accepts papers whose claims are supported by evidence and that interest some of its audience, and states that "novelty of the studied method is not a necessary criterion for acceptance" ([TMLR criteria](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html)). It rejects "bold statements unsupported by empirical or rigorous evidence" ([TMLR policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)). That matches a carefully controlled ablation with a noise floor and censored metrics, and it makes a clean negative result publishable. **It charges nothing.** Review is double-blind and open on OpenReview with at least three reviewers, and aims for a decision about nine weeks after submission ([TMLR FAQ](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/faq.html)). Submissions may be any length in the single-column tmlr.sty format, but main bodies over 12 pages take longer. arXiv posting is allowed at any time without linking from the submission. LLM use must be footnoted on the first page. Every author needs a complete OpenReview profile, and a per-author submission quota applies. It does not accept extended conference papers. The trade-off is standing: TMLR pages state no impact factor, and whether it is indexed in JCR or Scopus is unverified. If an Indian institutional metric requires a JCR-indexed journal, TPDS should be chosen instead.

**Second: IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems.** TPDS is the IEEE home for this topic. Its scope includes "fault resilience and performance measurements of multiple-processor systems" and runtime systems, and it has published Swift (2024), IceFrog (2025) and the DataStates-LLM journal version (2026). It has an impact factor of 5.9 and CiteScore of 15.2 ([Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=71)). A regular paper gets 12 double-column pages including references and bios, and up to 18 at submission. **Every final page beyond 12 costs US$220**, whether or not the paper is open access ([TPDS](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/write-for-us/15085); [CS guidelines](https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)). The traditional route is otherwise free. The hybrid open-access APC amount was not read. India falls outside IEEE's stated "low-income countries" waiver wording, the country discount excludes hybrid journals, and member discounts exclude students, so the author should assume no APC relief. Further requirements: a 100-200-word abstract with no maths or citations, appendices as supplementary files, mandatory ORCID, AI-generated content disclosed in the Acknowledgments, and an IEEE notice on any arXiv preprint. Review is single-anonymous by default with at least three reviewers, and double-anonymous on request with justification. TPDS also runs a reproducibility badge that requires a DOI-backed artifact ([TPDS reproducibility](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/misc/104303)). The risk is scale. TPDS reviewers would likely want a real multi-node migration over a network, measured drain times alongside the model, at least one model of 100M or more, comparisons against DataStates-LLM, Swift or PyTorch DCP, a policy driven by a real preemption trace, and sharded multi-GPU state. Without at least a real two-machine migration and a trace replay, it is a stretch.

**Third: IEEE Transactions on Cloud Computing.** TCC's scope explicitly lists "cloud economics" and "cloud performance and dependability" ([TCC](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/cc/cfp-ieee-transactions-cloud-computing)). It has published SDSP, on LLM serving over preemptible GPUs (2026), and elastic-DL resource management. It has an impact factor of 5.3 and CiteScore of 13.9 ([Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=6245519)). The page and cost rules match TPDS (12 pages, up to 18, US$220 per extra page), but **TCC offers no double-anonymous option** ([TCC](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/cc/write-for-us/15064)). Reviewers would judge the migrate/stay policy against real pricing and preemption data and would want an end-to-end cost result. It is the safer IEEE choice if the paper reads more as cloud-cost policy than as parallel systems.

## Open risks and what could not be verified

The largest technical risk sits in the project's own story about the transient, not in the literature. The optimizer note derives, as an inference rather than a sourced finding, that in PyTorch-style Adam the moments update *before* the step. With m = v = 0 and a large preserved t, the first update after resume is then already about lr·(1−β1)/√(1−β2)·sign(g) ≈ 3.16·lr on every coordinate, not about zero. The project's CLAUDE.md §7 says the first update is about zero and the spike arrives on the second step. This should be checked against `optim.py` before the transient is written up. The same derivation suggests that carrying t with zeroed moments effectively removes bias correction, leaving v under-filled by a factor of (1−β2^k). That inflates step size by about 10× at k = 10 and 3.2× at k = 100, fading over about 1,000 steps. If so, the "t is always carried" invariant may cause part of the penalty it is meant to isolate, and a `cold_reset` arm or a local bias-correction counter for rebuilt moments is needed to attribute it. The derivation further suggests that after 20 frozen warm-up steps with t preserved, m is about 88% formed while v is only about 2% filled, so the first real update could be about 7× the steady-state step. That would make naive re-estimation the worst protocol, and the fix to test is rescaling the rebuilt v by 1/(1−β2^20). These are hypotheses to test, consistent with Laing and Orvieto's "bias correction is an implicit schedule" view ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20516)), not established facts.

Several literature items remain uncertain. Mitra 2026, Zhang et al. 2026, Checkmate, CRIUgpu, ETC, SlimAdam and SkyNomad are arXiv-only, and their peer-review status is unknown. Topollai and Choromanska's EMNLP 2026 venue comes only from OpenAlex. ExCP's ICML oral status rests on the arXiv comment. Headline numbers for Gemini, PCcheck, gMig, Cricket and Anchor were not read from primary text, nor were repetition counts for Gemini, JIT and PCcheck. The arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and DBLP services were rate-limited or blocked partway through, so 2025-2026 systems-venue work may be under-covered. Items explicitly marked UNVERIFIED in the notes were kept out of this report and the bibliography: Schlögl et al. (NeurIPS 2023), Sia (SOSP 2023), Metis (ATC 2024), the Spotnik venue, the Delta-DNN and QD-Compressor author lists, and the OSDI and ATC venues of Gavel and HetPipe, which are cited as arXiv preprints only. Ash and Adams' NeurIPS 2020 venue was also unverified, and that paper was left out.

On devices, the Kaggle TPU generation (reported as v5e-8) and the Colab TPU generation are unverified, as are whether `torch_tpu` installs on either and whether Kaggle still requires phone verification. On journals, JCR quartiles were not read for any journal. TMLR's indexing is unverified, the TPDS hybrid APC amount and current submission URL are unconfirmed, and ACM and Elsevier requirements (APCs, India waivers, page limits, impact factors) come only from search snippets. Whether the author's institution participates in ACM Open is also unknown.

## Conclusion

The literature splits optimizer state cleanly into two worlds. Systems papers treat it as an indivisible payload to move faster. Optimizer papers treat it as a mostly redundant structure to keep smaller throughout training. Nobody has measured what happens when the state is cut at one boundary and the cut is priced. That makes the new paper's real contribution a *unit conversion*: it turns bytes not shipped into steps lost, measured against a noise floor, and steps lost into seconds and dollars under a notice window. The central experimental bet, that w_v behaves like `full` and w_m like `w_only` because of their horizons, is already half-predicted by ExCP's horizon argument and SlimAdam's SNR analysis. Confirming or refuting it at equal bytes is the cleanest single result the paper can offer.

The most consequential next step is not more literature. It is checking the bias-correction behaviour in `optim.py`, because the shape of the transient, and therefore the attribution of every StR number, depends on it. After that, a TMLR submission needs seeds with confidence intervals, a β2 sweep and one 124M-parameter cell. A TPDS submission additionally needs a real two-machine migration and a trace-driven policy evaluation.
