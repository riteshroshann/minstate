# Systems literature: fault-tolerant, preemptible (spot) and elastic DL training, and DNN checkpointing

Compiled 11 Nov 2026 for the minstate project. Every paper below was checked against at least one primary record (arXiv abs/API record, USENIX paper page, or the Crossref DOI record of the ACM/IEEE/Wiley publisher). Where only an index (OpenAlex) confirmed the paper, it is marked PARTIALLY VERIFIED. Citation counts come from OpenAlex ("OA") and Crossref ("CR"), both read on 10-11 Nov 2026. Counts for arXiv-only records cover only that record. Numbers in quotes or attributed to a paper are taken from its abstract or full text (PDF text extracted locally). Nothing here is from memory alone.

## Q1. Which systems train DNNs on spot/preemptible instances, what do they do at preemption, and does any of them question WHICH optimizer state must move?

### Takeaway
Spot-training systems (Varuna, Bamboo, Oobleck, Parcae, Lazarus, ReCycle, TrainMover, SkyNomad) all treat the full training state (weights plus optimizer state) as an indivisible unit. They keep it alive by replicating it, checkpointing it continuously, or migrating it whole, and they spend their design effort on parallelism reconfiguration and scheduling. None of them asks whether Adam's m and v need to survive the preemption. The schedulers (SkyPilot, Can't Be Late, SkyNomad, GFS) treat recovery as an opaque constant cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Varuna** (EuroSys'22, Microsoft Research India) trains on "low-priority" VMs "about 5x cheaper than dedicated GPUs", including a 200-billion-parameter model. It "morphs" pipeline depth × data parallelism to fit the available resources "without changing hyper parameters". For preemptions it "constantly checkpoints the model state across all stages… each layer is checkpointed independently", sharded across data-parallel replicas. For the largest model it "keeps the optimizer state in CPU". Reported: up to 18x end-to-end over other model-parallel approaches, up to 26% over other pipeline-parallel ones. — [arXiv 2111.04007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04007); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3492321.3519584)
- **Bamboo** (NSDI'23, UCLA/CMU/MSR/Princeton) puts *redundant computation* into pipeline bubbles: each node also computes its neighbour's layers, so a preempted stage can be taken over without a restart. It outperforms "traditional checkpointing by 3.7× in training throughput" and "reduces costs by 2.4×" vs on-demand. The full text notes that the memory overhead of redundancy comes from storing "intermediate results (activations and optimizer state)", and that the redundant computation works "with the same model parameters and optimizer states". — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi23/presentation/thorpe); [arXiv 2204.12013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12013)
- **Parcae** (NSDI'24, CMU/CUHK/UCLA/MSR) is proactive. It predicts availability with ARIMA, optimises "liveput" (expected throughput under possible preemptions) and does "lightweight instance migration". ParcaePS keeps a CPU-side copy of "parameters and optimizer states" updated from gradients, which "reduces communication by 5× for stateful optimizers (e.g., Adam)". Reported: up to 10× over prior spot systems, and 2.4× over Bamboo and Varuna on GPT-2 with 32 spot V100s. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/duan); [arXiv 2403.14097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14097)
- **Oobleck** (SOSP'23, Michigan/Peking) instantiates ≥ f+1 heterogeneous pipeline templates so that "already-replicated model states" give fast recovery. arXiv v2 (camera-ready, "figures and numbers are corrected") reports up to 29.6× over Bamboo and Varuna. The earlier abstract indexed by OpenAlex says 13.9×, so cite v2. — [arXiv 2309.08125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08125); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613152)
- **ReCycle** (SOSP'24, Stanford) handles failures without spare servers. It re-routes micro-batches to data-parallel peers and schedules into pipeline bubbles (decoupled backward pass, staggered optimizer step). — [arXiv 2405.14009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14009); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3694715.3695960)
- **Lazarus** (arXiv 2024, Berkeley/Michigan/Duke et al.) brings resilient, elastic MoE training to spot instances through adaptive expert replication and placement: "up to 5.7x under frequent node failures and 3.4x on a real spot instance trace". — [arXiv 2407.04656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04656)
- **SpotServe** (ASPLOS'24, CMU/PKU/CUHK) does LLM *serving* on preemptible instances. It uses Kuhn-Munkres matching to plan migration and "stateful inference recovery" that exploits the grace period ("e.g., 30 seconds for AWS spot instances"). — [arXiv 2311.15566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15566); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3620665.3640411)
- **SkyServe/SpotHedge** (EuroSys'25, Berkeley Sky): spot replicas spread across regions and clouds with on-demand fallback. It "reduces cost by 43% on average" and improves P50/P90/P99 latency by 2.3×/2.1×/2.1×. — [arXiv 2411.01438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01438); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689031.3717459)
- **SkyPilot** (NSDI'23, Berkeley) is an intercloud broker. It is the substrate for Can't Be Late. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi23/presentation/yang-zongheng)
- **Can't Be Late** (NSDI'24 Outstanding Paper, Berkeley) proposes the parameter-free "Uniform Progress" policy for switching between spot and on-demand under deadlines, evaluated on "three-month-long real spot availability traces on AWS" (open-sourced), with 27-84% savings on SkyPilot. Recovery is modelled as a *constant* "changeover delay d, which includes the time required to launch an instance, set up dependencies, and recover any potential progress loss caused by gaps between checkpoints". — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/wu-zhanghao); full text PDF nsdi24-wu-zhanghao.pdf
- **SkyNomad** (arXiv Jan 2026, Berkeley) schedules across multiple regions. It "accounts for migration cost" in a monetary cost model and reports 1.25-3.96× savings in real clouds, within 10% of optimal in simulation. — [arXiv 2601.06520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06520)
- **Tributary** (ATC'18, CMU) and **Proteus** (EuroSys'17, CMU) are the classic anchors for spot "tiered reliability". Tributary cuts cost for a given SLO by 81-86% vs non-preemptible scaling. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc18/presentation/harlap); [Proteus DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3064176.3064182)
- **TrainMover** (arXiv Dec 2024, Alibaba/Harvard/UT et al.) handles interruptions with standby machines and achieves "around 20 seconds of downtime… at the 1024-GPU scale". Its breakdown attributes 35.1% of the 4.45 min framework initialisation to "checkpoint loading… where model and optimizer states are restored from remote storage". — [arXiv 2412.12636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12636)
- **GFS** (ASPLOS'26, Alibaba/SJTU) is a preemption-aware scheduler with a spot quota for low-priority tasks: eviction −33.0%, queuing −44.1%, and "roughly $459,715 in monthly benefits" in a >10,000-GPU cluster. — [arXiv 2509.11134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11134); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3760250.3762231)
- Other 2024-2026 spot work: **Sailor** (ETH, heterogeneous geo-distributed dynamic clusters) [arXiv 2504.17096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17096); **RLBoost** (preemptible GPUs used for RL rollouts) [arXiv 2510.19225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19225); deadline-aware LLM fine-tuning with spot-price prediction [arXiv 2512.20967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20967); **AutoHet** (3D parallelism on heterogeneous spot GPUs) [arXiv 2512.20953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20953).
- An early measurement study: Li, Walls and Guo (ICDCS'20) measured distributed training on transient Google Cloud GPU servers ("three GPU types, six geographic regions, twenty CNNs"). — [arXiv 2004.03072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03072); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdcs47774.2020.00097)

### Inferences
- Across all spot-training systems, the unit that crosses a preemption is the complete (θ, m, v) state, whether replicated (Oobleck, ReCycle), shadowed on CPU (Parcae), recomputed redundantly (Bamboo) or checkpointed (Varuna). None treats the optimizer moments as optional or reconstructible. This is the gap minstate fills.
- Can't Be Late's constant `d` and SkyNomad's "migration cost" are exactly the slots minstate's bytes-vs-steps-lost curve can fill. minstate would make `d` a function of payload choice, bandwidth and notice window instead of a constant.

### Gaps
- Spotnik's venue could not be confirmed on a primary page (see catalogue).
- No primary page was fetched for TorchElastic or Elastic Horovod (they are software, not papers). SWIFT's text confirms that "CheckFreq and Elastic Horovod need to back up a copy of the model state (i.e., parameters and optimizer states) in memory" ([arXiv 2302.06173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06173)).

## Q2. DNN checkpointing systems: what they do and which metrics they report

### Takeaway
Checkpointing research has moved from frequency tuning (CheckFreq, 2021) to asynchronous multi-level I/O (DeepFreeze, DataStates-LLM, FastPersist, PCcheck), in-memory and peer checkpoints (Gemini, Checkmate, TierCheck), load-time resharding (ByteCheckpoint, UCP) and size reduction (Check-N-Run, Inshrinkerator, ExCP, BitSnap, LowDiff, MoC-System, MoEvement). The standard metrics are checkpoint stall/overhead (% of iteration time), checkpoint frequency achievable at bounded overhead, save/load time, recovery time or wasted GPU-hours, and ETTR (effective training time ratio). Compression papers add final-accuracy or downstream parity. No checkpoint paper reports how many *training steps* a lossy or reduced state costs after resume, measured against a deterministic control.

### Cited Findings
- **CheckFreq** (FAST'21, UT Austin/MSR): online-profiled iteration-level frequency, two-phase (snapshot then persist) checkpointing and a resumable data iterator. "Reduce the recovery time from hours to seconds while bounding the runtime overhead within 3.5%." — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast21/presentation/mohan)
- **Check-N-Run** (NSDI'22, Meta): differential (incremental) checkpointing of embedding tables plus quantization. Write bandwidth is reduced "by 6-17x" and capacity "by 2.5-8x" on production recommendation models. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi22/presentation/eisenman); [arXiv 2010.08679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08679)
- **DeepFreeze** (CCGrid'20, Argonne): asynchronous, distributed serialization and I/O, evaluated on Theta (CANDLE, ResNet). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ccgrid49817.2020.00-76)
- **Gemini** (SOSP'23, Rice/AWS): checkpoints to the CPU memory of peer machines, because remote-storage bandwidth limits recovery. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613145). Headline numbers were not read from a primary page.
- **Just-In-Time Checkpointing** (EuroSys'24, MSR India) checkpoints only *when a failure happens*, using surviving data-parallel replicas, so recovery replays "a single minibatch iteration". It cuts recovery cost "from several minutes to a few seconds per GPU, with nearly zero steady state overhead". — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3627703.3650085)
- **DataStates-LLM** (HPDC'24, Argonne): lazy asynchronous multi-level checkpointing. It exploits the fact that model and optimizer shards are immutable during forward and backward passes. "Up to 48× faster checkpointing and 2.2× faster end-to-end training", at up to 180 GPUs. A TPDS 2026 extension exists ([DOI 10.1109/tpds.2026.3694405](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpds.2026.3694405), index record only). — [arXiv 2406.10707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10707); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3625549.3658685)
- **ByteCheckpoint** (NSDI'25, ByteDance/HKU): parallelism-agnostic representation with load-time resharding. "Average reduction of 54.20×" in checkpoint stalls, and save/load "up to 9.96× and 8.80×". — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi25/presentation/wan-borui); [arXiv 2407.20143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20143)
- **Universal Checkpointing** (ATC'25, UIUC/Microsoft/Snowflake): decouples the checkpoint structure from the parallelism strategy for reconfiguration. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc25/presentation/lian); [arXiv 2406.18820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18820)
- **FastPersist** (arXiv 2024, Microsoft DeepSpeed): NVMe and parallel writes, "up to 116x faster than baseline", per-iteration checkpointing "with negligible overhead". — [arXiv 2406.13768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13768)
- **PCcheck** (ASPLOS'25, ETH): persistent concurrent checkpointing aimed explicitly at spot VMs, where frequent failures make frequent checkpoints necessary. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3669940.3707255)
- **Checkmate** (arXiv Jul 2025, MIT): multicasts gradients from switches to a CPU shadow cluster that replays the optimizer, giving per-iteration checkpoints at no slowdown. Reports "5 to 34.5x more frequent checkpointing", "80% to 97.1% reduction in repeated work per failure", and 1.3-6.5× throughput at equal frequency. — [arXiv 2507.13522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13522)
- **LowDiff** (SC'25, HUST): reuses (compressed) gradients as differential checkpoints. The arXiv version carries a different title. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3712285.3759891); [arXiv 2509.04084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04084)
- **Inshrinkerator** (SoCC'24, Georgia Tech et al.): dynamic non-uniform quantization of checkpoints plus quantization-aware delta compression. It notes that checkpoints "encompass the model's architecture, learned parameters, and optimizer state". — [arXiv 2306.11800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11800); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3698038.3698553)
- **ExCP** (ICML'24 oral per the arXiv comment, Huawei Noah's Ark): weight-momentum joint pruning plus non-uniform quantization, "approximately 70× compression for the Pythia-410M model" over 410M-7B models. The full text states that momentum states "are only weakly related to the parameters in the previous checkpoint after updating for hundreds to thousands of steps, especially for the first-order moment whose β1 = 0.9". — [arXiv 2406.11257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11257)
- **BitSnap** (arXiv Nov 2025, Tsinghua/SenseTime et al.): bitmask sparsification gives a "16x compression ratio without compromising model accuracy", and cluster quantization gives 2×. — [arXiv 2511.12376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12376)
- **MoC-System** (ASPLOS'25, HKUST-GZ): Partial Experts Checkpointing saves a subset of experts per checkpoint, for "up to a 98.9% reduction in overhead for each checkpointing process". — [arXiv 2408.04307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04307); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3676641.3716006)
- **MoEvement** (NSDI'26, Stanford): sparse expert snapshots plus upstream logging. Up to 4× lower checkpoint overhead and 31× lower recovery overhead, "ETTR ≥ 0.94 even under… MTBF as low as 10 minutes". — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/gandhi); [arXiv 2412.15411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15411)
- In-memory checkpointing for hybrid parallelism (HKUST-GZ et al.) — [arXiv 2310.12670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12670). **SWIFT** (TPDS'24, HKU/OneFlow) uses replica-based plus logging-based recovery, "up to 1.16x speedup in total training time" — [arXiv 2302.06173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06173); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpds.2024.3429625).
- 2026 work: **TierCheck** (local/peer differential plus remote base, "under 10s", models up to 40B) [arXiv 2605.17821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17821); **Leto** (in-place recovery on surviving hardware, 3.6-6.5× faster than checkpoint baselines on 6- and 72-GPU A100 clusters) [arXiv 2610.00687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00687); **FT-HSDP** (Meta, O(100K) GPUs: recovery stall cut "from 10 minutes to 3 minutes", effective training time "from 44% to 80%") [arXiv 2602.00277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00277); **LayerCheck** (CLUSTER'26, layer-wise selective persistence) [arXiv 2609.27193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27193).
- HPC-centre I/O studies: Gossman, Maurya, Nicolae and Calhoun (SCA/HPCAsia'26 workshops) microbenchmark liburing vs POSIX for LLM checkpoint/restore, covering aggregation, alignment and coalescing. — [arXiv 2512.24511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24511); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3784828.3784830)
- Fleet reliability: Kokolis et al. (HPCA'25, Meta) analysed "11 months of data from two… ML environments with 4 million jobs and over 150 million A100 GPU hours". They fit an MTTF model and introduce an Effective Training Time Ratio. — [arXiv 2410.21680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/hpca61900.2025.00096)

### Inferences
- The metrics minstate must report to be legible to this community are payload bytes, stall time and save/load time, recovery time and ETTR or wasted GPU-hours. minstate's StR × step_time maps onto "repeated work per failure" (Checkmate) and "ETTR" (Kokolis, MoEvement).
- The compression papers (Check-N-Run, Inshrinkerator, ExCP, BitSnap) validate with *end-of-training accuracy parity*. None reports a step-resolved recovery curve after the restore. That is minstate's methodological edge.

### Gaps
- Gemini's and PCcheck's quantitative claims were not read from a primary page (ACM full text not fetched).
- No paper found that checkpoints weights frequently and optimizer state rarely, or never, while measuring the training-progress cost.

## Q3. Elastic training and resizing: how is accuracy consistency defined and measured?

### Takeaway
There are two camps. Pollux, KungFu and Optimus adapt hyperparameters or resources and accept that the trajectory changes; they evaluate on job completion time and goodput. EasyScale, VirtualFlow and ElasWave insist that the trajectory be invariant to resizing. EasyScale's bar is *bitwise-identical parameters* versus a fixed-GPU DDP run. ElasWave reports a "convergence deviation" relative to an unchanged run. minstate's deterministic control plus seed band follows the EasyScale lineage.

### Cited Findings
- **EasyScale** (SC'23, Beihang/Alibaba): "elastic training should generate bitwise identical model parameters compared to the non-elastic DDP training over a fixed number of GPUs". It traces non-determinism sources across the stack (D1: deterministic algorithms; D2: heterogeneous deterministic kernels) and shows train-loss curves "identical" to DDP. As motivation it shows that TorchElastic (linear LR scaling) and Pollux give different ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 accuracies on 1, 2, 4 and 8 V100s "keep[ing] all hyper-parameters and random seeds as default". Deployment improved cluster utilisation by 62.1%. — [arXiv 2208.14228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14228); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3581784.3607054)
- **VirtualFlow** (Princeton/Google): "virtual node processing" decouples the batch from the hardware, enabling "reproducing training results across different hardware, resource elasticity, and heterogeneous training". — [arXiv 2009.09523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.09523)
- **Pollux** (OSDI'21, CMU/Petuum): "goodput" = system throughput × statistical efficiency. It reallocates resources and retunes batch size and LR, cutting average JCT by 37-50%. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi21/presentation/qiao)
- **KungFu** (OSDI'20, Imperial): adaptation policies change hyperparameters and system parameters (cluster size, sync strategy) online. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi20/presentation/mai)
- **Optimus** (EuroSys'18, HKU/ByteDance): a dynamic resource scheduler that uses online-fitted convergence models; OA 442 citations. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3190508.3190517)
- **Elan** (ICDCS'20, Tsinghua/SenseTime): generic elastic training. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/icdcs47774.2020.00018)
- **Tenplex** (SOSP'24, Imperial/Aalto): a parallelizable tensor collection (PTC) externalises dataset state and model state so that multi-dimensional parallelism can change at runtime. The arXiv v3 abstract calls the library "Scalai". — [arXiv 2312.05181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05181); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3694715.3695975)
- **ElasWave** (arXiv Oct 2025, HKUST-GZ/Huawei et al.) requires "parameter consistency… computation consistency", preserving global batch size and gradient scale and resharding RNG. On 96 NPUs: 1.35× throughput over ReCycle, 1.60× over TorchFT, and "convergence deviation is reduced by approximately 78%". — [arXiv 2510.00606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00606)
- **ETC** (arXiv Jul 2026, ICT-CAS; shares an author with EasyScale) does checkpoint-free peer-to-peer state migration for elastic Megatron-LM. It states that "it is necessary to migrate model parameters, optimizer states and other states", and reduces migration overhead by 2.33-6.37× vs checkpoint-based approaches. — [arXiv 2607.04749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04749)

### Inferences
- EasyScale's bitwise criterion is the strongest published consistency standard. minstate's §6 invariants (step-keyed data and RNG) are the same idea applied to migration. Citing EasyScale as the precedent for "the only thing that changes is the state" strengthens minstate's methodology section.
- Even ETC, the most recent "what to migrate" systems paper, takes the optimizer states as mandatory.

### Gaps
- No elastic-training paper reports the effect of resizing with *optimizer state discarded*.

## Q4. GPU process checkpoint/restore and live migration: state size and downtime

### Takeaway
Transparent GPU C/R (CRAC, Cricket, CRIUgpu, PhoenixOS, Singularity, gMig) snapshots *all* device memory, so its payload is at least the 16 B/param resident set (weights, grads, m, v) plus activations and allocator slack. Downtime runs from sub-second (concurrent C/R) to tens of seconds. These systems give the transparent "ship everything" upper bound that minstate's reduced payloads should be compared against.

### Cited Findings
- **Singularity** (Microsoft, arXiv 2022): every job is "preemptable, migratable, and dynamically resizable… resumed exactly from the point where the execution was preempted". "Migration and resizing latency… is reasonable (tens of seconds) with no repeat of computation", and "checkpoint sizes are comparable to user-level checkpointing". A naive swap of 32 GB V100 memory "would take 2-4 seconds". — [arXiv 2202.07848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07848)
- **CRIUgpu** (arXiv Feb 2025, Oxford/NVIDIA/Red Hat et al.) builds on the driver's cuda-checkpoint and supports CUDA and ROCm, "completely eliminating steady-state performance overheads". In-memory C/R on H100: GPT-2 Small takes on average "4.9 seconds" (lock+checkpoint) and restore "2.5 seconds"; GPT-2 XL (1.5B) takes "28 seconds" and "11 seconds". It evaluates BERT-B through LLaMA 3.1 8B on H100, A100 and V100. — [arXiv 2502.16631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16631)
- **PhoenixOS** (SOSP'25, SJTU IPADS) does concurrent OS-level GPU C/R using validated speculation (soft copy-on-write, recopy, on-demand restore), with "orders of magnitude higher performance than… NVIDIA cuda-checkpoint". As motivation: Llama2-13B inference holds 55 GB, so a stop-the-world C/R over 32 GBps PCIe 4.0 "takes at least 1.7 seconds". — [arXiv 2405.12079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12079); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3731569.3764813)
- **CRAC** (SC'20, Northeastern): CUDA C/R with streams and UVM, "approximately 1% or less" runtime overhead. — [arXiv 2008.10596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10596); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/sc41405.2020.00081)
- **Cricket** (CCPE 2021, RWTH Aachen): CUDA API-remoting virtualization layer with C/R. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpe.6474)
- **gMig** (IEEE TPDS 2020, SJTU/Intel/NUS): vGPU live migration with overlapped software dirty-page verification. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpds.2019.2947521)
- **On-demand and parallel C/R for GPU applications** (SoCC'24, SJTU) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3698038.3698510). **Anchor** (SOSP'26, Tsinghua) mitigates "GPU shallow disruptions with decoupled memory" — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830418.3843892); abstract not read.

### Inferences
- In minstate's B/param vocabulary, transparent C/R ships ≥ 16 B/param (it includes gradients), while application-level `full` ships 12 B/param. CRIUgpu's GPT-2 XL timings (28 s checkpoint, 11 s restore, in-memory) already approach a 30 s AWS notice window before any network transfer. That supports minstate's motivation, but phrase it as an inference.

### Gaps
- gMig, Cricket and Anchor downtime numbers were not extracted from primary text.

## Q5. Evaluation norms in the most important papers (to match in a new paper)

### Takeaway
Systems papers in this area mostly report throughput or cost ratios on 30-180 GPUs from trace replay, with few repetitions (Bamboo: 3 runs) and rarely any error bars. The ML-side near-misses (Mitra 2026; Zhang et al. 2026; Asadi et al. 2023) use 3-10 seeds with mean ± std. A paper that combines systems metrics (bytes, drain time, cost) with ML statistics (seed bands, censored StR, regret) would exceed the norm on both sides.

### Cited Findings

## Q6. Has anyone already measured "steps lost after dropping Adam moments at migration"?

### Takeaway
Not exactly, but there is one close near-miss that must be cited and differentiated. **Mitra (arXiv 2609.22087, June 2026)** measures the end-of-run perplexity penalty of discarding AdamW state at each interruption (weights-only checkpoints on power-constrained nodes) and tries reconstructing a decayed first moment. Unlike minstate, it does not measure steps-to-recovery or regret against a deterministic control, does not separate m from v or test factored/low-rank v, does not price bytes, and does not frame the problem as spot migration. Other near-misses compress moments with a first-update guarantee (Zhang et al., Oct 2026), reset moments on purpose (Asadi et al. 2023; Topollai & Choromanska 2026), or re-warm the LR when continuing pre-training (Gupta et al. 2023; Ibrahim et al. 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **Mitra 2026** (single author, arXiv only, 11 pages): "On a power- or downlink-constrained node the realistic choice is a weights-only checkpoint, so the optimizer moments are lost on resume. The question becomes how much of a… pre-gap moment to trust on resumption: 0× (zero-and-warmup), 1× (full preserve), or γ^g× (decayed)." Findings:
  - Checkpoint-Strong (full optimizer state, LR schedule indexed in effective time) "matches uninterrupted training to within data-ordering noise on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, and exactly on a GPT-2/AdamW task" (24.863 ± 0.019 perplexity both ways).
  - "Dropping AdamW state has a real cost (Elastic +0.76 short, +0.32 long perplexity)".
  - Reconstructing a decayed first moment "recovers only ≈21% of the penalty of discarding state on average (and not robustly across seeds)". The benefit "vanishes for eclipse-scale gaps".
  - Statistics: mean ± std over 3 (CIFAR) and 5 (GPT-2) seeds on one A100.
  — [arXiv 2609.22087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22087)
- **Zhang, Song, Yao, Zhang, Tianmiao 2026** ("Guarded Compression of Adam Optimizer State with Shared-Gradient Bounds", arXiv 2611.00589, posted 31 Oct 2026): accepts a compressed coordinate only if the restored first Adam update stays within tolerance t (in LR units). Guarded 8-bit log codes cut moment payloads by 69.5% (OPT-125M) and 71.9% (ResNet-18) vs FP32. "Every restored first update stays within tolerance." It measures the development-loss difference after 100-update continuations. The introduction frames checkpoints as how runs "survive failures, preemption and migration". It does **not** test dropping moments. — [arXiv 2611.00589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00589)
- **Topollai & Choromanska 2026** (NYU): quantized EMA optimizer states go "stale", and resets restore responsiveness. In a LLaMA-60M stress test, "second-moment stalling degrades convergence gradually, mainly through worse final loss". The extracted text truncates the first-moment comparison. OpenAlex lists the venue as EMNLP 2026; not confirmed on a publisher page. — [arXiv 2603.16731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16731)
- **Asadi, Fakoor, Sabach (NeurIPS'23, Amazon)**: resetting Adam's moments at each target-network update in Rainbow "significantly improves… performance… on the Atari benchmark". Averaged over 10 seeds on 12 games, and over all 55 games later. Here the loss changes, so stale moments are harmful, which is the opposite regime to minstate's. — [arXiv 2306.17833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17833); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-3164)
- **ExCP (ICML'24)** argues from horizon that first moments carry little information across hundreds of steps and prunes momentum jointly with weights. It does not drop it, and validates by final accuracy. — [arXiv 2406.11257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11257)
- **LR re-warmup**: Gupta et al. 2023 study re-warming when continuing Pythia-410M pre-training ([arXiv 2308.04014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04014), OA 120 citations), and Ibrahim et al. 2024 extend this to "re-warming and re-decaying" strategies ([arXiv 2403.08763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763), OA 135). Both are relevant to minstate's `lr_rewarmup` protocol. Whether they reset optimizer state was not checked.
- Systems that state the full optimizer state must move: ETC ("necessary to migrate model parameters, optimizer states") [arXiv 2607.04749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04749); Parcae's CPU-side "parameters and optimizer states" [arXiv 2403.14097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14097); SWIFT's description of CheckFreq and Elastic Horovod [arXiv 2302.06173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06173); TrainMover's restore breakdown [arXiv 2412.12636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12636).
- Searches returning nothing on point (arXiv API, Nov 2026): abs:"optimizer state" AND preemption; "optimizer states" AND "spot instances"; "without optimizer state" AND checkpoint; "optimizer states" AND "elastic training"; "optimizer state" AND lossy AND checkpoint.

### Inferences
- minstate's novelty claim should be stated precisely: *the first step-resolved (StR, regret against a bit-deterministic control with a seed-band floor) ablation of which Adam state components (θ, m, v, factored or low-rank v, embedding-only v, bf16 θ) must cross a preemption boundary, priced in measured bytes and fed into a notice-window/bandwidth/spot-price migration policy.*
- Mitra 2026 already shows that dropping AdamW state costs a measurable amount on GPT-2 and that simple decayed-moment reconstruction recovers little. minstate should cite it as concurrent, independent evidence and differentiate on four axes:
  1. metric: steps-to-recovery and regret, not final perplexity;
  2. component split: m vs v, factored v, embedding-only v;
  3. a destination-side frozen warm-up that rebuilds v from fresh gradients, rather than γ-decay of a cached moment;
  4. bytes, cost and policy.
- Zhang et al. 2026 is the closest *compression-side* competitor. minstate's 8-bit-moments arm should cite it, and ideally adopt its first-update error as an additional diagnostic.

### Gaps
- Mitra 2026 and Zhang et al. 2026 are recent arXiv-only preprints. Their peer-review status is unknown.
- I found no paper that runs `w_v` vs `w_m` (equal bytes, different horizons) or rebuilds v by frozen-weight gradient accumulation.

## (a) Ranked shortlist: the 5 papers most worth building on

1. **Mitra 2026 (arXiv 2609.22087)**. The only paper that measures the cost of discarding AdamW state at an interruption, with seeds and std. minstate must cite it as concurrent work and beat it on metric (StR and regret), component granularity (m vs v, factored v) and the bytes/cost policy.
2. **Can't Be Late (NSDI'24)**, with SkyNomad (2026) as the multi-region extension. It supplies the decision-theoretic frame and public AWS spot traces. Its constant changeover delay `d` is the hook: minstate turns `d` into a measured, payload-dependent quantity.
3. **Parcae (NSDI'24)**, with Bamboo (NSDI'23) for the evaluation template. It is the state-of-the-art spot-training system and the template for trace-replay evaluation, liveput and cost per token. Show that reduced-state migration shrinks Parcae-style migration cost.
4. **EasyScale (SC'23)**. Its bitwise-consistency methodology justifies minstate's deterministic control and seed band. Reviewers from systems venues know it.
5. **Guarded Adam-state compression (arXiv 2611.00589)**, with ExCP (ICML'24) as the venue-published precedent. These are the strongest moment-compression baselines. Put their payloads on minstate's Pareto plot and contrast "compress the moments" with "rebuild the moments".

## (b) Gaps where nothing was found
- No step-resolved (steps-to-recovery or regret) measurement of dropping Adam m and/or v at a migration or preemption, against a deterministic control.
- No equal-bytes contrast between shipping m only and v only (`w_m` vs `w_v`) in any systems or ML paper.
- No destination-side frozen-weight warm-up that rebuilds v from fresh gradients before resuming updates.
- No study of factored or low-rank v as a *migration payload* (Adafactor-style factorisation appears only as an optimizer design, outside this literature).
- No migration policy that trades payload bytes against training steps lost under a notice-window deadline. Spot schedulers treat recovery cost as a constant.
- No per-tensor-class (embedding vs other) analysis of optimizer-state importance at a restart.
- Repetition counts and error bars are largely absent from the systems papers (only Bamboo's three runs were found).
