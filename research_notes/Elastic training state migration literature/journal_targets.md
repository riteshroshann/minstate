## Q1. What does each candidate journal require? (scope, standing, format, review, cost, policies)

### Takeaway
The IEEE Computer Society transactions (TPDS, TC, TCC, TSC, TBD, TPAMI) all share one rulebook. A regular paper gets 12 double-column pages including references and bios, with a hard ceiling of 14 to 18 pages depending on the journal. Pages beyond 12 cost US$220 each (mandatory). The abstract must be 100 to 200 words, with no maths and no references. Appendices go in as supplementary files. ORCID is mandatory. Publishing is free on the traditional route, and open access is optional and paid. TMLR and JMLR charge nothing at all. Nature Communications charges a mandatory APC of US$7,350. Since 1 January 2026, ACM journals require an APC unless the author's institution is in ACM Open.

### Cited Findings

#### Rules shared by every IEEE Computer Society journal (TPDS, TC, TCC, TSC, TBD, TPAMI)
Source: [IEEE CS Author Guidelines](https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources), read 2026-11-11.
- Length: "All page limits include abstracts, references, and author biographies." A double-column page is "7.875″ x 10.75″ page with 9.5-point type and 11.5-point vertical spacing."
- Abstract: "Regular/special-issue paper – 100 to 200 words". Abstracts "must not include mathematical expressions or bibliographic references".
- Keywords: chosen from the ACM taxonomy in ScholarOne. "We recommend that you enter a minimum of three keywords."
- Template: "Using an article template is required for journal submissions." Authors are pointed to the IEEE Template Selector and the IEEE LaTeX Analyzer.
- Supplementary material is accepted, as separate files up to 350 MB. "All appendices in journal articles are considered supplemental material."
- ORCID: ScholarOne asks for one, and it is "required by all IEEE publications."
- Review model: "Unless a double-anonymous review is requested, each article undergoes a single-anonymous peer review process... We solicit peer reviews from at least three independent reviewers." Double-anonymous review must be requested with a justification. TCC and TC "do not offer this option".
- Acceptance rate: "Only about 25 percent of all manuscripts submitted are accepted." This figure covers CS publications as a whole, not each journal.
- Overlength charges (MOPC): the regular limit is "12 formatted pages for Transactions... Any pages or fraction thereof exceeding this limit are charged $220 per page." The charge applies whether or not the paper is open access. Waiver requests go to pubs-waivers@computer.org.
- Open access is hybrid. It is optional and paid after acceptance. "Corresponding authors from low-income countries are eligible for waived or reduced APCs." IEEE members get 5% off and society members 20%. The traditional route needs no payment.
- Author bios: "Author biographies are not required, and there is not a specific style format." However, the final-submission checklist includes a "biography of each author" and "photos of authors (for full-length journal articles)".
- Prior and conference versions must be disclosed. If you plan to send a conference version after submitting, "you must state your intention to submit in your cover letter". No other cover-letter content is mandated on this page.
- Concurrent submission to another venue leads to rejection.
- Portal: ScholarOne, with CS migrating to the IEEE Author Portal.
- Time to first decision: not stated ("The length of the peer review process varies").

## Q2. Fit and risk for this manuscript at each journal

### Takeaway
On scope, the manuscript is squarely inside TPDS, TCC and TMLR, and arguably inside TNNLS. The big risk everywhere except TMLR is scale. Recent TPDS work on DNN fault tolerance and checkpointing (Swift, DataStates-LLM) and on elasticity (IceFrog) evaluates on GPU clusters. A study capped at 25M parameters on one laptop GPU, with 7B figures labelled as extrapolations, will draw "does this hold at scale?" objections. TMLR's acceptance criteria explicitly downweight significance and novelty in favour of evidence-backed claims, which suits a carefully controlled ablation with a seed-band noise floor.

### Cited Findings
- TPDS publishes DNN failure recovery, checkpointing and elasticity work (Swift 2024, IceFrog 2025, DataStates-LLM 2026, DOIs above). Its scope names "fault resilience and performance measurements of multiple-processor systems" and "runtime systems". — [Xplore TPDS](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=71)
- TPDS evaluates reproducibility artifacts with attention to "the inherent complexities of parallel and distributed infrastructures". It recommends Chameleon because CodeOcean "cannot" run parallel and distributed systems. — [TPDS Reproducibility Author Instructions](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/misc/299908)
- TCC explicitly lists "cloud economics" and "cloud performance and dependability", and has published preemptible-GPU work (SDSP 2026). — [TCC CfP](https://www.computer.org/digital-library/journals/cc/cfp-ieee-transactions-cloud-computing)
- TMLR: "Papers should be accepted if they meet the criteria, even if the contribution or significance of the work is modest." Papers that "make bold statements unsupported by empirical or rigorous evidence" should be rejected. — [TMLR Editorial Policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- TNNLS full papers "must present a well-founded and conclusive treatment of a problem" and must not be "incremental". — [TNNLS Info](https://cis.ieee.org/publications/t-neural-networks-and-learning-systems/tnnls)
- JMLR: "Papers should report on what was learned in doing the work, rather than merely on what was done." Experiments "should be reproducible". — [JMLR Author Info](https://www.jmlr.org/author-info.html)
- NMI accessibility: "all papers are accessible to non-specialists". Articles are capped at 3,500 words. — [NMI formatting](https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/submission-guidelines/aip-and-formatting); [NMI content](https://www.nature.com/natmachintell/content)

### Inferences
What reviewers at each venue would likely demand that the manuscript lacks:

- **TPDS (moderate to high risk).** Reviewers would likely ask for:
  - real multi-node migration over a real network, not in-process simulation;
  - measured transfer and drain times alongside the analytic model;
  - at least one model at 100M parameters or more;
  - comparison against checkpointing and recovery systems (DataStates-LLM, Swift, and PyTorch DCP or torchrun elastic);
  - real spot-interruption traces to drive the policy (for example the public archive in FGCS doi:10.1016/j.future.2026.108827);
  - multi-GPU data-parallel or ZeRO-sharded state, where per-rank payload matters;
  - the 7B extrapolations either removed from the headline or backed by a scaling fit.

## Q3. Ranked recommendation

### Takeaway
1. **TMLR is the first choice.** It is the highest-standing journal where acceptance is realistic at the current scale, costs nothing, and its criteria match a rigorous negative-results-friendly ablation.
2. **IEEE TPDS is the best IEEE choice.** It is the IEEE flagship for this topic and has reproducibility badging, but it is a stretch unless multi-node experiments are added.
3. **IEEE TCC is the IEEE fallback.** Its explicit "cloud economics" scope suits the spot-preemption policy.

### Cited Findings
- TMLR charges no fees, uses double-blind open review, aims for a decision in about 9 weeks, allows arXiv, and does not require novelty. — [TMLR Editorial Policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html); [TMLR FAQ](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/faq.html); [TMLR Acceptance Criteria](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html)
- TMLR has accepted empirical and analytical Adam studies (OpenReview IDs xVEHiAZ7uR, sHSkJqyQgW, T1qXIDn9my).
- TPDS has IF 5.9 and CiteScore 15.2. It allows 12 pages (18 with MOPC) and offers double-anonymous review on request. It has published Swift, IceFrog and DataStates-LLM. — [Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=71); [TPDS Write for Us](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/write-for-us/15085); [CS guidelines](https://www.computer.org/publications/author-resources)
- TCC has IF 5.3 and CiteScore 13.9. Its scope includes "cloud economics", it offers no double-anonymous option, and it has published preemptible-GPU and elastic-DL papers. — [Xplore TCC](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/aboutJournal.jsp?punumber=6245519); [TCC Write for Us](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/cc/write-for-us/15064)

### Inferences
- **Why TMLR first.** The manuscript's strongest assets are the methodology: bit-deterministic controls, a seed-band noise floor, censored StR, regret area, and honest extrapolation labels. Those are exactly what TMLR's criteria reward. Its weakest asset, scale, is the thing TPDS reviewers weight most.
- **Trade-offs of TMLR.** It is single-column (tmlr.sty), not IEEE, and has no JCR impact factor on its pages. If a JCR-indexed IEEE journal is a hard requirement (for example for an Indian institutional metric), go with TPDS instead.
- **Why TPDS over TCC as the best IEEE choice.** Higher IF and CiteScore, closer topical precedent, the option to request double-anonymous review (useful for a sole undergraduate author), and a reproducibility badge that suits the existing artifact discipline. TCC is the safer IEEE fallback if reviewers judge the work more "cloud cost policy" than "parallel systems".
- **TNNLS.** It is higher-IF (9.7) but only fits if the paper is reframed around optimizer-transient analysis with theory, and the 10-page limit makes that hard. Treat it as a reframed alternative, not a top-3 pick.
- **Ruled out.** NMI and Nature Communications for significance and cost; TOCS and TACO for APC and scope; TPAMI for scope.
- **Dual submission.** IEEE CS rejects concurrent submissions, and TMLR forbids parallel archival submission. Submit to one at a time. arXiv posting is allowed by both.

## Q5. Experimental additions that would most raise acceptance odds at the first choice (TMLR), and what would make TPDS realistic

### Takeaway
For TMLR, the work needs statistical robustness and generality, not raw scale:
- more seeds with confidence intervals;
- a β2 and schedule sensitivity sweep;
- one model at 100M parameters or more;
- additional optimizers;
- a short formal treatment of the transient.

For TPDS, add at least one real two-node migration over a network and a real preemption trace.

### Cited Findings
- TMLR rejects "bold statements unsupported by empirical or rigorous evidence". The Reproducibility Certification values "additional baselines, analysis, ablations, or insights". — [TMLR Editorial Policies](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html)
- TPDS artifact reviewers assess "Documentation", "Completeness" and "Exercisability". TPDS points to Chameleon for distributed experiments. — [TPDS Reproducibility](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/misc/104303); [Author Instructions](https://www.computer.org/csdl/journal/td/misc/299908)
- A public multi-cloud spot-instance dynamics archive exists. — doi:10.1016/j.future.2026.108827 (FGCS, Crossref-verified)
- Prior checkpointing systems at TPDS, the obvious comparison points: DataStates-LLM doi:10.1109/TPDS.2026.3694405 and Swift doi:10.1109/TPDS.2024.3429625.

### Inferences
Ranked by value per unit of laptop effort. All of these are zero-cost.

1. **Seeds and uncertainty.** Report StR and regret with bootstrap confidence intervals over at least 5 seeds per arm at the headline cell, and report the share of censored (>W) runs. This answers the first question any TMLR reviewer will ask.
2. **β2 and β1 sensitivity.** Run, for example, β2 ∈ {0.95, 0.99, 0.999} with matching β1 variation. The w_v versus w_m horizon argument predicts a measurable shift, which turns a description into a tested hypothesis.
3. **One larger model.** GPT-2 small (124M) needs about 2 GB of resident fp32 Adam state, so it fits in 6 GB with bf16 autocast and gradient accumulation. Even one cell (full, w_only, w_factored_v at mid T) closes much of the scale objection.
4. **Optimizer breadth.** Add AdamW plus one other optimizer: Adafactor (factored v by design), Lion (single moment) or 8-bit Adam. This shows the finding is not Adam-specific.
5. **Transient analysis.** Derive the update-ratio spike, (1−β1)/√(1−β2), and bound the number of frozen-warmup steps needed to bring the preconditioner within ε of the carried v. Check that bound against the measurements.
6. **Baselines on the Pareto plot.** Plot published compression baselines (8-bit moments, rank-1 factored v) and a naive "full checkpoint at reduced frequency" against the policy.
7. **For TPDS only:**
   - a real two-machine migration over Ethernet or Wi-Fi, using gloo, the CRC wire format and measured drain time, compared with the analytic model;
   - a policy evaluation replayed on a public spot-interruption trace;
   - a free multi-GPU run, if any free tier (Kaggle 2×T4, Chameleon via an academic allocation) is accessible. Availability is UNVERIFIED.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Chameleon or other free testbeds grant access to Indian undergraduates.
